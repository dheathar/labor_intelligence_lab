Beveridge Curve — Greece 2010–2025
Vacancy rate (y) vs unemployment rate (x) · each dot = 1 year · curve shift = structural change
Scatter + path🔀 OJA+LFS
Data fields required
period (date, annual)unemployment_rate (float, Eurostat LFS)vacancy_rate (float, Eurostat JVS)
Source: Eurostat JVS (job vacancy rate) + lfsq_urgan (unemployment) · Annual 2010–2025 · Greece
Why this chart
The Beveridge curve is the canonical macro-labor chart — it distinguishes cyclical from structural unemployment. Movement along the curve is cyclical (recovery/recession). An outward shift of the curve signals structural mismatch: more vacancies coexisting with more unemployment than before. Greece's curve has shifted outward post-2020 — a matching efficiency problem.
Greece shows classic Beveridge curve dynamics: crisis-era movement to the right (2011–2013), then recovery back left. The 2022–2025 cluster at low-U / moderate-V suggests the market has tightened structurally — skills mismatch is now the binding constraint, not lack of demand.
Labour Market Tightness by Occupation
Vacancy / Unemployment ratio · all 18 ISCO occupations · Q2 2025 · >1 = employer's market
Horizontal bar🔀 OJA+LFS
Data fields required
occupation (ISCO-08 text)vacancy_count (integer, OJA proxy)unemployed_count (integer, LFS)tightness_ratio (float, derived)
Source: Cedefop OJA as vacancy proxy + Eurostat LFS occupation unemployment · Q2 2025
Why this chart
V/U ratio by occupation separates genuine skill shortages (high V, low U) from general unemployment concentrations. A ratio >1 means more vacancies than unemployed in that field — employers compete for candidates. <0.5 means many more unemployed than openings — a supply-side problem. The vertical reference line at 1.0 makes the comparison instant.
Data Scientists show V/U ratio of 2.5+ — severe shortage. Warehouse Operatives and Admin Assistants have ratios below 0.3 — structural surplus that cannot be easily redirected to shortage occupations without significant reskilling investment.

Why this chart
