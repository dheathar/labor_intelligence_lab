Why this chart
A bubble chart encodes 4 variables simultaneously: x-axis (salary), y-axis (demand growth), bubble size (market volume), and color (skills gap severity). Roles in the top-right are high-demand, high-paying, fast-growing — the hardest to hire. This view lets a hiring manager instantly prioritize budget and sourcing effort.
Data Scientists are top-right with large gaps — consider talent pipelines (university partnerships, reskilling) rather than pure market hiring.

Supply–Demand Balance by Occupation
Diverging bar — positive = labour surplus, negative = labour shortage · 2025 Q2
Diverging bar
Why this chart
A diverging bar chart uses a zero-baseline to show both direction (surplus vs shortage) and magnitude in a single glance. Traditional side-by-side bars require mental subtraction; diverging bars pre-compute the gap. The axis label "surplus ← → shortage" orients any reader instantly — no legend required.
Nursing shows the most critical shortage (−34%). Data Science is in significant shortage despite rapid supply growth — demand is simply outpacing graduates.
Skills Profile Radar — Demand vs Available
Top 8 skills for Data Scientist role · 0–100 scale
Radar / spider
Why this chart
A radar chart is uniquely suited for comparing two multivariate profiles on identical axes. The area between the two polygons is the skills gap — the larger the gap area, the more intensive the training intervention needed. Skills where the supply polygon extends beyond demand (e.g. SQL) indicate over-training; shrink that and redirect resource.
LLMs/GenAI shows the largest gap (72 demand vs 22 supply). This is the highest-priority training investment for any Data Science workforce plan.

Sankey
Why this chart
A Sankey diagram is the canonical choice for flow data where the width of links encodes volume. It answers "where do graduates go?" and reveals structural mis-alignments (e.g. engineering graduates not entering tech) and unexpected talent pools (e.g. biology graduates entering data science). No other chart type shows multi-to-multi flows readably.
~30% of Engineering graduates enter non-engineering roles. This is a hidden talent pool for ICT sector hiring — target reskilling, not just new enrollment.
Workforce Change Decomposition (2020–2025)
Net employment change = new entrants − exits + transitions in − transitions out
Waterfall
Why this chart
A waterfall chart decomposes a net change into contributing factors, each shown as a positive or negative bar running from the prior cumulative value. It answers "why did employment in Software Development grow by X?" in a single image — impossible to do with a pie or standard bar. Essential for identifying which lever (graduation rates, retention, immigration) is most impactful to pull.
Inbound career transitions (from adjacent roles) now account for ~28% of software developer headcount growth — larger than university graduation inflows.

Skills Co-occurrence Network
Node size = demand frequency · edge weight = co-occurrence in same posting · color = cluster
Force graph
Why this chart
A force-directed network graph is the only chart type that reveals both individual skill importance (node size) and skill-cluster topology simultaneously. Clusters that emerge organically from co-occurrence data correspond to real job families. Bridge nodes (between clusters) identify the most versatile, high-value skills. No bar chart or heatmap can show graph topology.
Python is the central bridge between the AI/ML cluster and the Data Engineering cluster — the single highest-leverage skill investment across occupations.
Skill Ranking Evolution (Bump Chart)
Rank by % of postings mentioning skill · 2020–2025
Bump chart
Why this chart
A bump chart plots rank (not absolute value) on the Y-axis against time. This is superior to a standard line chart for ranking data because: absolute values vary in scale; crossing lines are legible at rank resolution; and the chart explicitly answers "which skill overtook which?" — the key research question. LLMs/GenAI rising from rank 8 to rank 2 in 5 years is a research finding, not just a data point.
LLMs/GenAI is the fastest-rising skill in the dataset (+6 rank positions). Cloud overtook SQL between 2021–2022 — a structural shift from on-premise to cloud infrastructure.
Skill Demand Volume — Taxonomy Treemap
Rectangle area = % of postings mentioning skill · grouped by ESCO skill cluster
Treemap
Why this chart
A treemap uses rectangular area to show part-of-whole relationships across a hierarchy in limited screen space. Where a pie chart can handle ~6 slices before it becomes unreadable, a treemap handles 30–50 items. It respects the hierarchical structure of ESCO (skill group → skill) naturally. Color encodes the parent cluster; size encodes demand volume.
Digital & ICT skills account for 43% of total skill demand volume — larger than all other ESCO groups combined. This finding drives national digital skills strategies.
Skill Demand Growth vs Wage Premium
X = YoY demand growth (%) · Y = wage premium over occupation median (%) · bubble = supply volume
Scatter + regression
Why this chart
A scatter plot with regression line tests the hypothesis that scarce, growing skills command wage premiums. The regression line shows the average relationship; outliers above the line are "underpriced" skills (high demand growth, unexpectedly low premium) — a finding for wage researchers. Bubble size adds supply volume to show whether premia reflect genuine scarcity or legacy pricing.
LLMs/GenAI and Cloud show both high demand growth AND high wage premium — classic scarcity pricing. Excel shows flat demand growth but persistent premium — grandfathered legacy skill.

Employment Rate — Small Multiples by Country
Ages 20–64 · % employed · 2015–2025 · ELSTAT / Eurostat LFS
Small multiples
Why this chart
Small multiples (same chart type repeated at small scale for each country) allow direct cross-country comparison without the overplotting that a single multi-line chart produces with 6+ series. Each panel has identical axes, so the reader's eye can scan for shape differences (slope, volatility, recovery speed) rather than decoding a tangle of overlapping lines. Standard practice in Eurostat and OECD publications.
Greece shows the steepest recovery slope post-2015 but remains 8pp below EU average. WB6 countries (Serbia, Montenegro) show accelerating growth from a lower base — a structural catch-up pattern.

Why this chart
The bubble matrix is used for strategic portfolio analysis. Risk (x), scale of impact (bubble = employment), and economic weight (color = wage) are the three policy-relevant dimensions. Roles in the top-right with large bubbles represent high-automation-risk, high-employment occupations — the priority target for reskilling policy. Roles bottom-left are policy tailwinds (safe + growing).
Logistics Coordinators: high automation risk (62/100), large employment share, moderate wages — the single highest-priority occupation for reskilling investment in the next 5 years.
Employment by Contract Type — Stacked Area
Full-time permanent · Part-time · Fixed-term · Self-employed · 2015–2025 Greece
Stacked area
Why this chart
A stacked area chart shows composition (the proportional mix) AND total volume trend simultaneously — two charts compressed into one. For labor market policy, the changing mix of contract types is as important as the headline employment rate. A rising share of fixed-term contracts alongside rising total employment is a warning signal that growth is precarious. The baseline of zero makes both absolute and relative changes visible.
Fixed-term contracts grew from 11% to 19% of total employment 2015–2022 — growth is real but increasingly precarious. Post-2022 stabilization may reflect legislative intervention (EU Directive on transparent working conditions).
Youth NEET Rate — Annotated Timeline
Ages 15–29 · Not in Employment, Education or Training · % · with key policy events
Annotated line
Why this chart
An annotated timeline overlays policy event markers on a KPI trend line, making causal reasoning explicit. Without annotations, a reader can only observe "the rate fell after 2016" — with annotations, they can see "the rate fell 3pp following the Youth Guarantee expansion." This is the standard format in OECD policy effectiveness reports and Cedefop monitoring publications. Annotations encode qualitative context directly in the visual.
Greece peaked at 28% NEET rate in 2013 (EU's highest). Three visible inflection points align with Youth Guarantee rollout, ESF co-funded apprenticeships, and post-COVID stimulus. Post-2022 rate (16%) still 4pp above EU-27 average.

Salary Distribution by Occupation & Experience
Box = IQR · whiskers = 10th–90th percentile · dot = median · EUR gross annual
Box plot
Why this chart
A box plot is the most information-dense single chart type for a distribution: it shows the median (line), interquartile range (box), tails (whiskers), and outliers (dots) in one compact symbol. A single salary figure ("average: €52k") is misleading — knowing the full distribution (e.g. "50% earn between €35k–€65k, but top 10% earn €88k+") is actionable career information. Multiple grouped boxes enable cross-occupation comparison.
The wide IQR for Data Scientists (€42k–€78k) reflects high experience-premium — worth the investment in skills progression. Nurses show compressed distribution — wages are institutionally set and negotiation headroom is limited.
Career Transition Flows
Annual occupational moves · flow width = % of movers · based on LFS panel data
Sankey
Why this chart
A Sankey diagram encodes career transition volume as link width — the most natural mapping for flow data. The left side shows "leaving occupation" and the right side shows "entering occupation." Wide links indicate high-probability transition paths. For career counseling, this chart replaces anecdote ("I heard developers often move into data science") with empirical evidence ("45% of developers who change occupation enter data science").
Marketing Manager → Data Scientist is a surprisingly large flow (18% of MM changers). The shared analytical and communication skills make this a viable transition path with targeted upskilling.
Demand Outlook 2025–2028 with Uncertainty
OJA volume forecast · shaded = 80% confidence interval · dashed = baseline scenario
Line + CI band
Why this chart
A line chart with confidence interval band makes uncertainty explicit rather than hiding it behind a single point forecast. For career decisions, knowing that Data Scientist demand will "probably be 20–35% higher in 2028" is more useful than a spuriously precise "will be 27% higher." The widening band over time correctly represents increasing uncertainty. This is the standard for Cedefop Skills Forecast (2025–2035) publications.
All five occupations show positive demand outlook through 2028. Data Scientist has both the highest expected growth AND the widest uncertainty band — reflecting ongoing GenAI disruption of the role definition.
Your Skills vs Market Demand — Gap Radar
Illustrative: candidate skills profile vs top 8 skills demanded for Data Scientist role
Radar / spider
Why this chart
A personal skills radar gives the job seeker an immediate gestalt of their readiness profile. The area enclosed by the "your skills" polygon versus the "market demand" polygon is the total gap. Unlike a table of scores, the radar shows which gaps are large (require significant upskilling) versus small (easily bridged). It is also the most common self-assessment visualization in career development tools (LinkedIn, Coursera, etc.) — users already know how to read it.
LLMs/GenAI and Deep Learning are the largest gaps. Python and SQL are strong — leverage these as your entry point while upskilling on AI. Target 3 months of structured GenAI training to close the most critical gap.
