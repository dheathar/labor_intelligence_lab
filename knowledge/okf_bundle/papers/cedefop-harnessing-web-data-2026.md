## Abstract

Follow-up to the 2025 Brussels conference, held 28–29 May 2026 at Cedefop premises in Thessaloniki.
Features 16 presentations across 4 sessions covering: skills & qualifications linking (NQF, ESCO),
regional/sectoral OJA analysis, occupation classification algorithms, green & digital transition skills,
AI/GenAI impacts on skill demand, career pathway identification, skills ecosystem mapping, and
gender gaps in skills intelligence.

## Key findings

- OJAs are the primary real-time signal for labour market skills intelligence across EU
- Multilingual NLP + ESCO mapping is the standard pipeline for occupation/skills extraction
- Green skills demand measurable via 591 labelled ESCO concepts + NLP lexicon extension
- GenAI is reshaping skill demand profiles at occupation level — measurable via OJA time series
- Career pathways can be mined from sequential OJA patterns at individual-employer level
- Regional skills ecosystems (NUTS-2) require combining OJA with LFS and admin data
- Gender bias detectable via lexicometric analysis of job posting language
- {'Skills-vacancy–training provision gap analysis': 'critical for WBalkans (Serbia, North Macedonia)'}
- {'Ukrainian refugee employment matching': 'OJA demand × admin records supply-side'}

## LIVLAB relevance

Directly informs EU-ALMPO WP4 methodology. UOP's own Panagiotis Zervas presented on multilingual
