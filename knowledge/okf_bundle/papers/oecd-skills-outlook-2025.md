## Abstract

Examines how skills connect to labour market opportunities across OECD countries.
Analyses skill instability trends, socio-economic disparities in skill development,
and policy levers for aligning education with labour demand.

## Key findings

- 39% of existing skill sets will be transformed/outdated by 2030 (down from 44% in 2023)
- {'Skill instability slowing': '57% (2020) → 44% (2023) → 39% (2025)'}
- Socio-economic background is primary driver of skill development opportunity gaps
- Gender differences persist in skill usage and reward structures
